Exact MAP Inference for Elastic Spatial Alignments on Directed Acyclic Graphs: A Probabilistic Tree-DTW Framework

Abstract
Traditional dynamic programming models for spatial alignment, such as Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) and Dynamic Time Warping (DTW), strictly operate on one-dimensional sequential topologies. This constraint fundamentally limits their application in geospatial routing and mobility analysis, where physical networks frequently branch and merge. This paper presents a probabilistic generalization of Tree-DTW, reformulating the deterministic alignment problem as a Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) inference task on a Tree-Structured Markov Random Field (MRF). By utilizing Log-Domain Belief Propagation, we demonstrate that optimal point-to-point map matching across directed acyclic graphs can be achieved in linear time. We highlight the practical implications of this framework in the context of urban mobility and acoustic sensor networks, where multi-modal signal dispersion necessitates rigorous topological consistency.

1. Introduction

The alignment of noisy, continuous state sequences to a discrete topological space is a foundational challenge in geospatial data engineering. Standard map-matching pipelines heavily rely on the Viterbi algorithm or DTW to fuse sensor observations (e.g., GPS, inertial odometry) with a spatial network. However, these models inherently assume a linear sequence of observations.

When modeling complex phenomena—such as a fleet of vehicles diverging into separate delivery routes, or acoustic energy profiles propagating through an urban city grid—the underlying spatial structure is not a sequence, but a directed acyclic graph (DAG). Applying linear sequence models to branching data inevitably results in "junction collapse," where independent spatial trajectories are averaged into a single, physically impossible hypothesis.

This paper formalizes Tree-DTW within a probabilistic graphical framework. By treating the source topology as a directed Markov Random Field, we establish a mathematically exact method for handling splits (forks) and merges (joins) without structural degradation, enabling sophisticated continuous sensor fusion.

2. Methodology: The Tree-Structured MRF

2.1 Graphical Model Definition

Let the source structure be a directed acyclic graph \( G_A = (V_A, E_A) \) satisfying the strict tree property: no undirected loops exist, ensuring that independent branches never cross-contaminate. The target environment is defined as a directed network \( G_B = (V_B, E_B) \).

For every source point \( a \in V_A \), we define a hidden random variable \( X_a \in V_B \). The objective is to identify the optimal joint configuration \( \mathbf{X}^* \) that maximizes the posterior probability of the alignment:

\[ P(\mathbf{X}) \propto \prod_{a \in V_A} \psi_a(X_a) \prod_{(p,c) \in E_A} \phi_{p,c}(X_p, X_c) \]

2.2 Unary and Pairwise Potentials

The model factorizes across the tree cliques using two primary potentials:

  • Unary Potential (Emission Model) \( \psi_a(X_a = v) \): The localized observation likelihood, derived from sensor probability densities (e.g., acoustic signal strength or Kalman filter states) mapped to spatial coordinates.
  • Pairwise Potential (Transition Model) \( \phi_{p,c}(X_p, X_c) \): A structural prior enforcing graph monotonicity and connectivity. The potential is \( 1 \) if the transition from \( X_p \) to \( X_c \) is valid in \( G_B \) (allowing for 1:N spatial warping), and \( 0 \) otherwise.

3. Log-Domain Belief Propagation

Given the tree property of \( G_A \), Max-Product Belief Propagation converges to the exact global optimum in two linear sweeps. To mitigate floating-point underflow over extended paths, inference is executed in the logarithmic domain.

3.1 Upstream Pass (Handling Merges)

A topological sweep from sources to sinks computes the forward message \( \log D[a][v] \), encapsulating the optimal upstream joint probability terminating at \( X_a = v \):

\[ \log D[a][v] = \log \psi_a(v) + \sum_{p \in A_{\text{pred}}(a)} \max_{x \in \text{reach}(v)} \Big( \log D[p][x] + \log \phi_{p,a}(x, v) \Big) \]

At a merge node, the summation over independent predecessors represents the multiplication of conditional probabilities, guaranteeing that upstream paths coalesce optimally at a single physical location.

3.2 Downstream Pass (Handling Splits)

A reverse topological sweep computes the backward message \( \log B[a][v] \), representing the conditional probability of all optimal downstream futures:

\[ \log B[a][v] = \log \psi_a(v) + \sum_{s \in A_{\text{succ}}(a)} \max_{w \in \text{reach}(v)} \Big( \log B[s][w] + \log \phi_{a,s}(v, w) \Big) \]

4. Traceback and MAP State Extraction

The extraction of the exact sequence relies on the conditional independence of tree structures. For a parent split node \( C \) with branches \( A \) and \( B \), the downstream variables are strictly independent given the state of \( C \): \( P(X_A, X_B \mid X_C) = P(X_A \mid X_C) P(X_B \mid X_C) \).

The MAP extraction algorithm leverages this property through an ordered top-down traversal:

  1. Anchor Commitment: Highest-level forks are locked via max-marginalization:
    \( X_s^* = \arg\max_{v} ( \log D[s][v] + \log B[s][v] - \log \psi_s(v) ) \).
  2. Downstream Propagation: Split children filter their target space based on the committed parent state, safely searching the \( B \) table without conflict.
  3. Upstream Propagation: Merged branches are resolved instantaneously by reading cached structural back-pointers from the \( D \) table.

5. Applications in Urban Mobility and Acoustic Networks

The probabilistic Tree-DTW framework is uniquely suited for multi-modal urban mobility analysis. A primary application lies in the deployment of acoustic sensor networks for microscopic traffic counting. When vehicle acoustic signatures propagate through urban corridors, the signal profile inherently splits across building intersections and merges at roundabouts or confluences.

By mapping acoustic energy densities to the Unary Potential \( \psi \), and utilizing the physical road grid (such as OpenStreetMap data) as the target network \( G_B \), this framework aligns the branching acoustic topologies cleanly to the map. The probabilistic formulation ensures that spatial noise is rigorously marginalized, enabling highly accurate flow analysis and map-matching that traditional HMMs cannot natively support.

6. Conclusion

By shifting Tree-DTW into a Tree-Structured Markov Random Field, we provide a robust, mathematically rigorous pipeline for hierarchical spatial alignment. The model guarantees strict topological compliance, avoids phantom optimization artifacts, and achieves exact MAP inference in \( O(|V_A| \times |V_B|) \) time, offering a critical tool for advanced geospatial data engineering.
